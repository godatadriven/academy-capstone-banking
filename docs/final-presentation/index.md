# Final Presentation

This page outlines the brief of what to include in your final presentation. This is a **recommendation and expectation**, not a rigid template - you can deviate if you have good reason to, but sticking to this structure is the safest way to make sure you cover everything you'll be assessed on.

## Choice of tool

You should present your project using PowerPoint, although another slide tool is fine if preferred.

If you do not create a slide deck, you should meet with your program manager and/or mentor to discuss how your proposed solution will be presented instead, and confirm it still meets the brief.

## Slide breakdown

From here, it is assumed you have chosen to create a presentation. Your presentation should be made up of the following ~14 slides:

1. **Title slide** - project name, use case, team members.
2. **Use case overview** - the business problem you were asked to solve, and the concrete question you turned it into.
3. **Business scoping** *(~3 slides)* 
    - stakeholders mapping
    - driver tree / hypothesis tree and opportunity sizing
    - GenAI go/no-go decision with justification
4. **Insights (EDA / Modeling)** *(~3 slides)* 
    - key data-driven insights 
    - modeling approach
    - modeling problem type + model choice
    - key metrics and evaluation
    - validation (including any pitfalls avoided, e.g. leakage or overfitting).
5. **Dashboard overview** *(~2 slides of screenshots or a live demo)* 
    - walk through your Power BI dashboard
    - KPIs being tracks
    - How it supports the story .
6. **Limitations & assumptions** *(~1 slide)* 
    - what you assumed, what you couldn't fully verify, and where the analysis or model is weakest. 
7. **Final proposal and call-to-action** *(~1 slide)* 
    - what it would take to move this into production (data needs, access/roles, cost/sustainability considerations).
    - your recommendation, translated into financial/operational impact and risk, and what you'd want the stakeholder to decide or approve.
8. **Questions** 
    - Here you can plan a break for questions from the audience.
9. **Appendix: process & production path**  
    - links to your repo/documentation

## Things to keep in mind

- Every slide should earn its place. If a slide doesn't change what the audience believes or decides, cut it or fold it into another.
- Follow the pyramid principle: lead with your conclusion/recommendation up front (not just at the end), then support it.
- Assume your audience is a mix of technical and non-technical stakeholders - translate technical detail into business language throughout, not just on the proposal slide.
