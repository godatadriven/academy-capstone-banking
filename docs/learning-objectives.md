# Learning Objectives

These are the learning objectives from your six-month bootcamp journey. The capstone is designed to **integrate and demonstrate** them together - they are not a separate checklist to complete alongside the project, but the underlying skills the project itself is built to exercise.

Each section below is mapped to the [Project Guide](project-guide/index.md) stages where it's most directly applied.

## 1. Data Engineering Basics

- Understand and recognise the end-to-end data engineering lifecycle, and where your work fits within it.
- Apply basic engineering principles: modularity (reusable code), version control (conceptual understanding of Git), reproducibility, logging and error handling.
- Understand why data quality is crucial for business impact, and design simple checks to catch it.
- Apply best practices to make an analysis or model closer to production-ready.
- Recognise the need for different roles and responsibilities when engineering AI solutions (data scientist, data analyst, engineer).

**Applied primarily in:** [Assess Data Quality](project-guide/04-assess-data-quality.md), [Build the MVP](project-guide/08-build-the-mvp.md), [Consider Production Readiness](project-guide/09-production-readiness.md), [Technical Documentation](deliverables/technical-documentation.md)

## 2. Data-Driven Communication

- Apply the pyramid principle in presentations to deliver a strong message.
- Create a storyboard and use the storyboarding method to build pyramid-proof stories.
- Present a pyramid-proof story in a way that is easy to follow.
- Adapt communication style and content based on the technical expertise of the audience.

**Applied primarily in:** [Build the Story](project-guide/13-build-the-story.md), [Build the Power BI Dashboard](project-guide/12-power-bi-dashboard.md)

## 3. Structured Opportunity Identification

- Understand the importance and power of sizing opportunities.
- Identify drivers to break business problems into smaller, more tangible pieces.
- Define concrete, actionable and testable business hypotheses to validate opportunities and explore solution directions.
- Develop a driver tree and hypothesis tree to capture and pursue a business opportunity.

**Applied primarily in:** [Frame the Problem](project-guide/01-frame-the-problem.md), [Size the Opportunity](project-guide/02-size-the-opportunity.md)

## 4. Explorative Data Analysis (EDA) Techniques

- Approach exploratory data analysis in a structured and systematic way.
- Practice data-driven exploration and iterative analysis, including distributions, correlations, segmentations and outlier analysis.
- Decide when to use Python versus SQL to perform data transformations.
- Translate a business question into relevant variables and initial research questions.
- Translate insights into new hypotheses and follow-up questions.
- Understand the difference between data-driven hypotheses and assumption-based hypotheses.
- Translate insights into possible business explanations and their impact.
- Identify data issues such as missing values, bias and inconsistencies.
- Critically assess your own analysis, including bias, unknowns and possible misinterpretation.
- Clearly document results in a structured way, with logical flow, assumptions and findings.
- Understand the role of EDA in downstream modelling and stakeholder communication.

**Applied primarily in:** [Understand the Data](project-guide/03-understand-the-data.md), [Assess Data Quality](project-guide/04-assess-data-quality.md), [Explore the Data](project-guide/05-explore-the-data.md)

## 5. Data-Driven Visualisation - Power BI

- Translate a business question into appropriate visualisations and a clear dashboard structure.
- Build a dashboard following a clear structure based on the pyramid principle.
- Design dashboards that are simple, contain no unnecessary visuals, and focus on key insights.
- Apply key Power BI features such as drill-down, drill-through, filters and basic data model structures.
- Understand how dashboards are used for reporting, decision-making and compliance.
- Assess whether a visualisation is correct and whether its context is complete.
- Use dashboards to tell a story and convince stakeholders.
- Distinguish when Power BI is the right tool, and when an alternative solution is more appropriate.

**Applied primarily in:** [Build the Power BI Dashboard](project-guide/12-power-bi-dashboard.md)

## 6. Business-Driven AI Modelling

- Translate a business question into a problem type (classification, regression, forecasting) and select an appropriate modelling approach.
- Justify model choice based on performance metrics (accuracy, recall, precision) and business requirements (e.g. false positives vs. false negatives).
- Translate metrics into financial impact, risks and operational consequences.
- Recognise common pitfalls: data leakage, overfitting, incorrect validation.
- Structure a data science project using an MVP approach, iterative improvement, and clearly defined milestones.
- Assess when GenAI is or isn't suitable, evaluating use cases on value, risk and feasibility.
- Explain models in business terms, and support decision-making.
- Determine whether a model is sustainable, considering data availability, maintenance cost and scalability.

**Applied primarily in:** [Build the Model](project-guide/06-build-the-model.md), [Quantify Business Impact](project-guide/07-quantify-business-impact.md), [Build the MVP](project-guide/08-build-the-mvp.md), [Assess GenAI](project-guide/10-assess-genai.md), [Assess Sustainability](project-guide/11-assess-sustainability.md)

## Why this mapping matters

If you find yourself unable to demonstrate one of these objectives clearly in your finished project, that's a signal - go back to the mapped stage(s) and strengthen that part of the work, rather than treating the objective as a separate box to tick. See [What Success Looks Like](overview/what-success-looks-like.md) for how these objectives connect to overall project quality.
